## 2. "About"

# About

In 1515 King Manuel I decided to test whether the elephant and the rhinoceros really were sworn enemies. The animals were let into the courtyard of a Lisbon palace in front of a crowd. The elephant took one look at its opponent and ran away.

Hence our name: **O Elefante Fugiu**, "The elephant ran away". We collect stories like this from Portugal's past: short, checked, and tied to a place you can visit today. Each one takes a minute to read.

## Foi aqui. It happened here

A story sticks better when you're standing on the very spot. That's why every story has a point on the map and a "bridge to today": a church, a square, a museum or a monument where it left a trace.

## How to read

Every story has three layers. Stop at any of them.

- **Hook.** One line: "The king set a rhino against an elephant. The elephant ran away."
- **Story.** How it happened, in a few lines.
- **More.** What was going on around it, how we know, and where to go.

You can read by era, by thread (the sea, food and wine, power and revolt, people like us, nature) or simply on the map.

## Who it's for

- For people who love history but not textbooks.
- For travellers who want to know what happened right where they're standing.
- For families. The "With kids" button in the header hides stories about war, executions and repression.

## Can you trust it

We don't pass legends off as facts. Every story carries a label: "Documented", "Legend" or "Disputed", and a list of sources at the bottom. How we work is explained in "Method and sources".

## What's here so far

The project is growing. Three eras are ready: "Avis and the Age of Discovery", "Pombal, the earthquake and the court's flight" and "Estado Novo and the Carnation Revolution", [N] stories in all. Six more eras, from Roman Lusitania to the present day, are in the works.

## Who we are

O Elefante Fugiu is an independent project: free, ad-free and tracking-free. [Name, one line about yourself and why Portugal.] We're looking for historians, archaeologists and guides willing to check a whole era. If that's you, get in touch.

## 3. "Method and sources"

# Method and sources

History is full of beautiful inventions, and we love them too. That's why we always say plainly what has been proven and what hasn't.

## Three labels

- **Documented.** The event is confirmed by documents of the time or by historians' work.
- **Legend.** The story has been told for centuries, but there's no proof. We retell it and explain what's true in it.
- **Disputed.** Historians disagree or the sources contradict each other. We say exactly what the dispute is about.

## Where our facts come from

First of all from period documents, academic work, archives and the official websites of museums. Every story has at least one source, and major events have at least two. They are all listed at the bottom of the story, next to the date it was last checked.

## Dates and numbers

When sources disagree, we give both versions or say "about": "1778 or 1782". Army sizes and death tolls are almost always estimates, so we give a range instead of one neat number.

## Children and difficult topics

Most stories are fine for any age and have no label. The 12+ label marks stories with battles or cruelty. Before stories about repression, persecution and death we give a warning and ask whether you want to continue. Those stories have no jokes and no elephant. The "With kids" button in the header hides them altogether.

## Images

Every illustration is credited with its author and licence. If an image was made with AI, the caption says so, so that nobody mistakes it for a period document.

## Mistakes

They happen. If you find one, tell us via "Found a mistake?". We'll fix the text and update the date it was checked. The story's link doesn't change, so you can keep sharing it.

## Languages

Stories are written in Portuguese and translated into English and Russian. If a translation isn't ready yet, we show the English or Portuguese text and say so.

## What's stored in your browser

So that the site remembers your choices, your browser saves: language, colour theme, "With kids" mode, the last era you opened and the last story from "Surprise me". This data stays in local storage and is never sent anywhere. You can delete it in your browser settings by clearing this site's data.

## Who else is involved when you read the site

The site itself collects nothing about you, but it doesn't run in a vacuum. These services see your IP address:

- **GitHub Pages** (GitHub, Inc.) serves the site's pages and may log IP addresses under its own policy.
- **The map server** (OpenStreetMap or the provider named in the map's corner) receives requests when you view the map.

Links to "How to get there", sources and museum websites lead to other sites with their own rules.

We check every story, but history is a field of debate, and mistakes are possible. This site does not replace scholarly sources. Museum opening hours and prices change: check them on the official website before you go.
